# M4 Historical Atlas v0

The atlas is generated by [`scripts/build_atlas.py`](../scripts/build_atlas.py) from [`m4-quarterly-v1.csv`](../data/processed/m4-quarterly-v1.csv). It creates 20 figures under [`results/atlas-v0/figures`](../results/atlas-v0/figures), machine-readable tables, and figure-level provenance metadata.

Regenerate it with:

```bash
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/m4-mpl MPLLOCALDIR=/tmp/m4-mpl PYTHONPATH=src python3 scripts/build_atlas.py
```

The figures are intentionally plain and descriptive. Every figure has a corresponding entry in [`figure-metadata.json`](../results/atlas-v0/figure-metadata.json) specifying variable definition, units, frequency/transformation, data vintage, and limitations.

## Main findings

- The four recorded commodities represented 85.5% of total exports in 2025; the residual is explicitly labelled residual merchandise exports, not non-mining exports.
- Coal’s implied annual export unit value is approximately $38/t in 2015–2016, $172/t in 2021, $204/t in 2022, $128/t in 2023, $104/t in 2024, and $64/t in 2025.
- Construction growth matches the Phase 1A-R QA values: +20.3% (2019), −3.2% (2020), −22.8% (2021), +8.2% (2022), +3.1% (2023), +14.0% (2024), and +15.9% (2025).
- Construction’s average real share is highly seasonal: Q1 1.15%, Q2 2.97%, Q3 3.01%, Q4 6.13%.
- Overlapping annual growth rates agree closely across the official GDP vintages when only complete years are compared. The 2005/2010 overlap is shorter (2011–2013) than the 2010/2015 overlap (2016–2020); NSO metadata do not establish why revisions differ.
- Agriculture shows large contractions/recoveries and clear seasonality, supporting a distinct descriptive block.
- Construction has a plausible but not decisive case for separation; missing GFCF, employment, and exchange-rate data limit the conclusion.

Construction-block classification: **STILL UNDECIDED**.

## Seasonality recommendation

Do not seasonally adjust in this phase. For later work, raw quarterly values with explicit quarterly dummies are the safest default for GDP, agriculture, construction, and capex. YoY quarterly growth is useful for vintage comparisons; selected fiscal/external blocks may be summarized annually where quarterly seasonality obscures the question.
